Automated speech recognition (ASR) is being adapted to the highly safety-critical aviation environment, where misheard or misunderstood communications can have serious consequences. A key challenge is recognizing airline callsigns accurately, especially when speech is noisy, unclear, or includes previously unseen words.

## Two Complementary ASR Approaches

Researchers from A*STAR's Institute for Infocomm Research (I²R) and AIR Lab have developed two complementary methods tailored to different operational needs:

1. **Offline ASR Enhancement**
   - Target use: post-operations analysis, offline transcription, and non–time-critical tasks.
   - Performance: **77% relative reduction in callsign error rate**, achieving **98.97% accuracy**.
   - Benefit: near-human reliability for callsign recognition when real-time constraints are relaxed.

2. **Real-Time ASR Enhancement**
   - Target use: live air traffic control (ATC) and cockpit environments where latency is critical.
   - Current performance: **23.96% error reduction**, with **91.94% accuracy**.
   - Projected performance: **57.07% error reduction** with planned API and system improvements.
   - Benefit: more reliable callsign recognition during live operations, directly supporting safer and faster decision-making.

## Leveraging Combined Expertise

A*STAR's Institute for Infocomm Research (I²R) and AIR Lab have developed specialized automated speech recognition (ASR) enhancements to improve the reliability of airline callsign recognition in safety-critical aviation communications.

## Collaboration and Expertise

The work combines:

- **A*STAR I²R (Aural and Language Intelligence team):** Research methodology, ASR model design and development, and speech/language processing expertise.
- **AIR Lab:** Real ATC audio data, operational and domain expertise from active air traffic controllers, and infrastructure to evaluate solutions in realistic operational settings.

## Impact on Aviation Safety

Enhanced callsign recognition helps mitigate known communication risks between pilots and controllers by:

- Reducing read-back/hear-back errors.
- Lowering the chance of misidentifying flight numbers.
- Decreasing controller cognitive load, particularly in high-traffic or complex sectors.

## Next Steps

Future work will focus on continuing to improve real-time performance and bringing these capabilities closer to operational deployment.
